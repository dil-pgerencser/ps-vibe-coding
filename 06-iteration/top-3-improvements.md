# Top 3 Improvements by Impact

1. Remove the simulation / failure scenario links (highest impact)
A peer explicitly flagged this and it directly undermines your core hypothesis. You're building trust through verification signals — then exposing scaffolding that signals "this is a demo." Any buyer who sees a "simulate failed booking" link will mentally reclassify the product as unfinished. Trust is the product; anything that breaks the illusion tanks conversion before badges get a chance to work.

2. Add context to the metrics banner (high impact)
"What are the numbers at the top?" means your primary trust mechanism — the social proof numbers — is confusing rather than reassuring. A number without a label is noise. A number with a clear label ("94% of clients re-hire", "verified in 48h") is the signal that moves a hesitant buyer. This is the core feature of your hypothesis; if it's not readable, the hypothesis can't be tested.

3. Fix mobile layout (medium impact)
Marketplace browsing skews mobile. A broken mobile layout means a large slice of your real target audience (buyers evaluating an unfamiliar provider) has a degraded first impression — exactly when you need the verification signals to land clearly. This won't generate your first validation data, but it unblocks it.

The real next step: Get 20–30 real buyers to view a zero-review provider profile and track whether they click "Book" or exit. Without that, you're optimizing a product whose core hypothesis is still unmeasured.
